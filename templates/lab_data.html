1. Exploratory Data Analysis

Variable Correlation Matrix

Pearson correlation between input variables (DMSO, Trehalose, SFB) and viability.

Interpretation: DMSO shows moderate negative correlation with viability (r = −0.562), consistent with dose-dependent toxicity. Trehalose shows near-zero linear correlation (r = 0.048), reflecting a non-linear relationship that both models capture directly from the raw concentration values, without any hand-engineered polynomial features. SFB's positive correlation (r = 0.622) is associated with the composition of the formulations: SFB is the balance/diluent component (DMSO + Trehalose + SFB = 100% in 198/{{ dataset.get('n_total') }} rows; 8 rows = 10%) and was not included among the predictors.

Data Distribution

Histogram of post-thaw viability across all {{ dataset.get('n_total') }} experimental observations.

Interpretation: The viability distribution is bimodal, with peaks near 0% and near 90–100%, reflecting the binary nature of cryopreservation outcomes: cells largely either survive the process well or do not survive it at all, with comparatively few intermediate results.

3. Neural Network (PyTorch) Performance

A deep neural network was trained using PyTorch on the two raw cryoprotectant concentrations (% DMSO, trehalose) directly — no polynomial feature engineering. The non-linear toxicity relationship is captured by the network itself rather than by hand-built features. The architecture uses 3 hidden layers (128→64→32 neurons) with ReLU activations, regularized by the configuration selected on validation loss alone: {% set wc = nn_metrics.get('winning_config') %}{% if wc.get('use_bn') %}Batch Normalization{% else %}no Batch Normalization{% endif %}, {% if wc.get('dropout') > 0 %}dropout {{ wc.get('dropout') }}{% else %}no dropout{% endif %}, {% if wc.get('weight_decay') > 0 %}weight decay (L2, {{ wc.get('weight_decay') }}){% else %}no weight decay{% endif %}, and early stopping on a dedicated validation set. The output layer is a single linear neuron, so the raw network output is not bounded; the predictions shown by the application are limited to the physical range of viability, 0–100%. Training happens in PyTorch; in production, predictions are served by a NumPy-only reimplementation of the trained network, so the deployed application does not depend on PyTorch.

Figure 1: Effect of DMSO Alone

Interpretation: Viability of HepG2 cells frozen with increasing DMSO concentrations. Cells were frozen with 0–100% DMSO, thawed after 7 days, and viability assessed via trypan blue exclusion and MTT assays. Data represent mean ± SD from 6–15 samples per concentration (n=1–3 independent experiments). Statistical analysis used two-way ANOVA with Sidak post-test. Asterisks denote p<0.0001; "ns" indicates non-significant.

Figure 2: Effect of Trehalose Alone

Interpretation: Viability of HepG2 cells frozen with increasing trehalose concentrations. Cells were frozen with 20–100% trehalose, thawed after 7 days, and viability assessed using trypan blue exclusion and MTT. Data show mean ± SD from 4–5 samples per concentration (n=1–2 independent experiments).

Figure 3: Combined Effect (DMSO + Trehalose)

Interpretation: Viability of HepG2 cells frozen with combinations of dimethyl sulfoxide and trehalose. Cells were frozen with 5% or 10% DMSO (D) and 5–30% trehalose (T). After 7 days, cells were thawed and viability was measured by trypan blue exclusion and MTT assays. Values represent mean ± SD from 2–7 samples per concentration (n=1 independent experiment).
