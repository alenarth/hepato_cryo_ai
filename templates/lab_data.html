1. Exploratory Data Analysis

Variable Correlation Matrix

Pearson correlation between input variables (DMSO, Trehalose, SFB) and viability.

Interpretation: DMSO shows moderate negative correlation with viability (r = −0.574), consistent with dose-dependent toxicity. Trehalose shows near-zero linear correlation (r = 0.003), reflecting a non-linear relationship captured by polynomial feature engineering. SFB's apparent positive correlation (r = 0.655) is confounded rather than causal: SFB acts as a balance/diluent variable (DMSO + Trehalose + SFB = 100% in 208/216 rows; 8 rows = 10%), and including it adds negligible predictive value to the RF model while inflating SFB's apparent feature importance at DMSO's expense. SFB is therefore excluded from the feature set.

Data Distribution

Scatter plots of each cryoprotectant versus viability and histogram of viability distribution.

Interpretation: DMSO shows a clear toxicity threshold — viability drops sharply above ~25%. Trehalose displays a non-linear pattern: moderate concentrations are beneficial but excessive amounts are detrimental. The viability distribution is bimodal, with peaks near 0% and near 90–100%, reflecting the binary nature of cryopreservation outcomes.

2. Random Forest Algorithm Performance

Model Fit (Actual vs. Predicted)

Scatter plot of observed versus AI-predicted viability on the test set.

Interpretation: Points clustered along the diagonal confirm the model's high R² = 0.980 and low error (RMSE = 5.02%), demonstrating accurate generalization to unseen data.

Survival Zones (Heatmap)

Predictive mapping of all DMSO × trehalose concentration combinations.

Interpretation: Lighter areas indicate optimal cryopreservation zones. The toxicity threshold is visually apparent where excessive cryoprotectant concentrations lead to a sharp drop in estimated cell viability.

Learning Curve

Model performance as a function of training set size.

Interpretation: Converging training and cross-validation curves confirm the model is not overfitting and that the dataset size is sufficient for reliable generalization.

Residuals Analysis

Distribution of prediction errors across predicted values.

Interpretation: Residuals scattered randomly around zero with no systematic pattern confirm the model has no significant bias across the full range of viability predictions.

Feature Importance

Relative contribution of each cryoprotectant to the Random Forest predictions.

Interpretation: DMSO dominates (~77%) the model's predictions, consistent with its strong dose-dependent toxicity. Trehalose contributes ~23%, reflecting its complementary but non-linear role in cryopreservation outcomes.

{% if nn_available %}4{% else %}3{% endif %}. Empirical Foundation (HepG2 Cells)

Figure 1: Effect of DMSO Alone

Interpretation: Viability of hepatocytes frozen with increasing DMSO concentrations. HepG2 cells were frozen with 0–100% DMSO, thawed after 7 days, and viability assessed via trypan blue exclusion and MTT assays. Data represent mean ± SD from 6–15 samples per concentration (n=1–3 independent experiments). Statistical analysis used two-way ANOVA with Sidak post-test. Asterisks denote p<0.0001; "ns" indicates non-significant.

Figure 2: Effect of Trehalose Alone

Interpretation: Viability of hepatocytes frozen with increasing trehalose concentrations. HepG2 cells were frozen with 20–100% trehalose, thawed after 7 days, and viability assessed using trypan blue exclusion and MTT. Data show mean ± SD from 4–5 samples per concentration (n=1–2 independent experiments).

Figure 3: Combined Effect (DMSO + Trehalose)

Interpretation: Viability of hepatocytes frozen with combinations of dimethyl sulfoxide and trehalose. HepG2 cells were frozen with 5% or 10% DMSO (D) and 5–30% trehalose (T). After 7 days, cells were thawed and viability was measured by trypan blue exclusion and MTT assays. Values represent mean ± SD from 2–7 samples per concentration (n=1 independent experiment).
