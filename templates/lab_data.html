1. Exploratory Data Analysis

Variable Correlation Matrix

Pearson correlation between input variables (DMSO, Trehalose, SFB) and viability.

Interpretation: DMSO shows moderate negative correlation with viability (r = −0.574), consistent with dose-dependent toxicity. Trehalose shows near-zero linear correlation (r = 0.003), reflecting a non-linear relationship that both models capture directly from the raw concentration values, without any hand-engineered polynomial features. SFB's apparent positive correlation (r = 0.655) is confounded rather than causal: SFB acts as a balance/diluent variable (DMSO + Trehalose + SFB = 100% in 208/216 rows; 8 rows = 10%), and including it adds negligible predictive value to the RF model while inflating SFB's apparent feature importance at DMSO's expense. SFB is therefore excluded from the feature set.

Data Distribution

Histogram of post-thaw viability across all 216 experimental observations.

Interpretation: The viability distribution is bimodal, with peaks near 0% and near 90–100%, reflecting the binary nature of cryopreservation outcomes: cells largely either survive the process well or do not survive it at all, with comparatively few intermediate results.

3. Neural Network (PyTorch) Performance

A deep neural network was trained using PyTorch on the two raw cryoprotectant concentrations (% DMSO, trehalose) directly — no polynomial feature engineering. The non-linear toxicity relationship is captured by the network itself rather than by hand-built features. The architecture uses 3 hidden layers (128→64→32 neurons) with ReLU activations, regularized with weight decay (L2, 1e-4) and early stopping on a dedicated validation set — no Batch Normalization or Dropout are used. Training happens in PyTorch; in production, predictions are served by a NumPy-only reimplementation of the trained network, so the deployed application does not depend on PyTorch.

Figure 1: Effect of DMSO Alone

Interpretation: Viability of hepatocytes frozen with increasing DMSO concentrations. HepG2 cells were frozen with 0–100% DMSO, thawed after 7 days, and viability assessed via trypan blue exclusion and MTT assays. Data represent mean ± SD from 6–15 samples per concentration (n=1–3 independent experiments). Statistical analysis used two-way ANOVA with Sidak post-test. Asterisks denote p<0.0001; "ns" indicates non-significant.

Figure 2: Effect of Trehalose Alone

Interpretation: Viability of hepatocytes frozen with increasing trehalose concentrations. HepG2 cells were frozen with 20–100% trehalose, thawed after 7 days, and viability assessed using trypan blue exclusion and MTT. Data show mean ± SD from 4–5 samples per concentration (n=1–2 independent experiments).

Figure 3: Combined Effect (DMSO + Trehalose)

Interpretation: Viability of hepatocytes frozen with combinations of dimethyl sulfoxide and trehalose. HepG2 cells were frozen with 5% or 10% DMSO (D) and 5–30% trehalose (T). After 7 days, cells were thawed and viability was measured by trypan blue exclusion and MTT assays. Values represent mean ± SD from 2–7 samples per concentration (n=1 independent experiment).
